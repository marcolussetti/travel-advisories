## Entry requirements

### **Visa/Passport**

Irish citizens can travel to Chile without a visa if their stay does not exceed 90 days.

On arrival, migration authorities will provide you with a tourist card (a thin receipt-like document) with PDI (Policía de Investigaciones de Chile) printed on it. This card is valid for a maximum period of 90 days. You must keep this card and present it to immigration officers upon departure from Chile. Failure to do so may result in delays.

If your tourist card is lost or stolen, you may obtain a new one by applying online (Spanish only) – [**here**](https://serviciomigraciones.cl/en/home/). Alternatively, well in advance of departure, you may go to a special police unit (Jefatura Nacional de Migración y Policia Internacional) in Santiago or to the nearest PDI office outside of Santiago.

You can request an extension of stay for up to 90 days online. You must do so before your current tourist card’s expiration date. A fee applies.

If you stay in Chile for longer than the allowed period, you will have to pay a fine at the Chilean immigration office before you can leave the country. Please consult Chile’s National Migration Service for further information (Spanish only) – [**here**](https://serviciomigraciones.cl/en/home/).

Passports must be valid for a minimum period of 6 months from the date of entry into Chile and have at least 1 completely blank page.

It is advisable to take a number of photocopies of your passport and to also carry a photocopy of your passport at all times.

Chile has strict requirements for the entry and exit of minors (under the age of 18). All minors departing Chile must provide airport officials with a notarised authorisation from non-travelling parents and an original apostilled birth certificate. For non-travelling parents based in Chile, this notarisation can be done by a Chilean notary public.

**NOTE**: Any children who were born in Chile will require a Chilean passport to exit Chile.
