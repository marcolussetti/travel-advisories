# Safety and security

## Safety and security

### Ongoing hostilities throughout Sudan

Since April 2023, there has been heavy fighting throughout the country between the Sudanese army, a paramilitary group, and other affiliated armed groups.

The Sudanese army controls the eastern part of the country, including Khartoum and Port Sudan, while paramilitary groups control the western part. However, territorial control frequently shifts along multiple frontlines.

Drone attacks occur frequently and without warning nationwide, including in areas controlled by the Sudanese army, such as Khartoum and Port Sudan.

The security situation remains highly volatile and violent clashes are likely to continue. The civilian population has been deliberately targeted by violence, including sexual violence. Foreigners and employees of international organizations have also been targeted by these attacks.

The conflict has severely damaged infrastructure and disrupted essential services, including medical care, banking services, electricity and telecommunications. Access to basic necessities such as food, water, medication, fuel and cash is extremely challenging. Hostilities have caused mass population displacements within the country and the region, resulting in a dire humanitarian situation.

Port Sudan New International Airport is the primary civilian airport operating international flights. Khartoum’s international airport has reopened, but international flights remain limited. Both airports are subject to drone attacks and may close without notice.

If you decide to leave the country by air, you should carefully assess the security situation before heading to the airport. All travel in Sudan is extremely dangerous due to ongoing fighting.

If you are in Sudan and choose to stay despite this advisory:

* monitor local and international media to stay informed on the evolving situation
* use your judgment to decide the best time and the safest means to move to a safer location if you think it's required
* shelter-in-place until it is safe to leave the country
* make sure you have sufficient supplies of food, water and fuel
* keep your doors and windows locked at all times and ensure your phone is always charged
* ensure that your passport and other travel documents are up-to-date and secure at all times
* sign up for the [Registration of Canadians Abroad](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/registration) service to receive important safety updates from the Government of Canada

[Canada’s response to the crisis in Sudan](https://www.international.gc.ca/world-monde/issues_development-enjeux_developpement/response_conflict-reponse_conflits/crisis-crises/sudan-soudan.aspx?lang=eng)

### Demonstrations

Prior to the current conflict, large-scale demonstrations took place to demand the establishment of a democratically elected government. Security forces used excessive and lethal force to disperse crowds, which resulted in numerous casualties. They arrested many activists and protestors. There continue to be reports of arbitrary arrests and detention in Khartoum and across the country, regardless of the presence of demonstrations.

Even peaceful demonstrations can turn violent at any time. They can also lead to disruptions to traffic and public transportation.

* Avoid areas where demonstrations and large gatherings are taking place
* Follow the instructions of local authorities
* Monitor local media for information on ongoing demonstrations

[Mass gatherings (large-scale events)](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/mass-gatherings)

### Border crossings

The land borders with some neighbouring countries are closed. Border closures may occur without notice.

Border areas are extremely hazardous due to cross-border armed groups’ activity, disputed territory, banditry, smuggling, and landmines.

Do not attempt to cross land borders.

### Crime

Crime has increased substantially following several years of armed conflict. Violent crime is most prevalent in regions affected by conflict. However, it can occur anywhere in the country.

Incidents include:

* murders
* armed robberies
* burglaries
* home invasions
* carjackings
* assaults, including sexual assaults

### Random searches

Sudanese authorities may conduct random searches of personal belongings, including those of employees of international organizations.

### Terrorism

There is a threat of terrorism throughout the country, including in Khartoum and Port Sudan. Terrorist attacks could occur at any time.

Targets could include:

* government buildings, including police stations and military bases
* diplomatic missions and offices of non-governmental organizations
* schools, including universities
* places of worship
* airports and other transportation hubs and networks
* public areas and tourist attractions, including:

+ restaurants
+ coffee shops
+ shopping centres
+ markets
+ hotels and other sites frequented by foreigners

Always be aware of your surroundings when in public places.

### Kidnappings

There is a risk of kidnapping throughout the country, including of foreign nationals and aid workers.

If you travel to Sudan despite this advisory:

* seek professional security advice
* arrange for personal security
* change your travel patterns regularly
* be aware of your surroundings
* avoid displaying signs of affluence
* always travel in a group

### Outages, shortages and service interruptions

#### Fuel

Sudan is experiencing severe fuel shortages that affect a wide range of services, including but not limited to:

* ground and air transportation
* electricity
* telecommunications, including access to the Internet

#### Power

Power outages are frequent nationwide. Not all buildings are equipped with generators.

Power outages could impact essential services, including:

* street lighting
* traffic lights
* refrigeration in restaurants and supermarkets
* the supply of fuel at gas stations
* the public water supply

#### Communications

Cellular coverage is unreliable and connectivity remains an issue in several parts of the country. Internet and mobile phone services may be unavailable for prolonged periods.

You should not rely on your mobile phone in case of emergency.

* Avoid travelling alone
* Inform a friend or family member of your itinerary

You should also save or print key information in case of an Internet disruption, including:

* your travel itineraries
* contact details of the [Emergency Watch and Response Centre](http://travel.gc.ca/assistance/emergency-assistance) in Ottawa
* emergency numbers

### Roads

Overland travel is risky due to the ongoing armed conflict. Permits are often needed to travel from one city to another. Many checkpoints, both official and illegal, are set up throughout the country. Vehicles are regularly stopped and searched, often leading to violent incidents. Movement through checkpoints may be delayed or denied.

Driving standards and vehicle maintenance are poor throughout the country. Traffic accidents occur frequently. Pedestrians are often involved in accidents.

You should avoid all overland travel across Sudan, even if you are with an experienced guide. If you still decide to do so, make sure you have the following essential equipment and supplies:

* a shovel
* traction ramps for sand
* a GPS device
* reliable means of communication
* spare fuel and tires
* water and food supplies
* cash

#### Road conditions

Road conditions are generally poor. Major roads are paved, while most secondary roads are not. Many roads outside the capital are sand tracks.

A four-wheel-drive vehicle is required for overland travel, except on the Khartoum–Kassala–Port Sudan, Khartoum–Atbara, and Khartoum–El Obeid highways.

Driving conditions may be hazardous during the rainy season from July to September.

Roads can be dangerous due to:

* artillery fire and other risks of military activity, including drone strikes
* unexploded ordnance and landmines
* potholes
* a lack of traffic signs
* numerous motorcycles, bicycles, pedestrians, livestock and stray animals on the road
* insufficient lighting and vehicles not using their headlights

### Public transportation

You should avoid public transportation due to the risks posed by the armed conflict and drivers’ recklessness.

### Women's safety

Women travelling alone may be subject to harassment or verbal abuse.

* Avoid travelling alone, especially after dark or in isolated areas
* Dress in accordance with local customs to reduce unwanted attention:
  + dress conservatively with loose-fitting clothing
  + avoid wearing short skirts or shirts with low necklines
  + cover your arms

[Advice for women travellers](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/advice-for-women-travellers "Advice for women travellers")

### 2SLGBTQI+ persons

Sudanese law criminalizes sexual acts between individuals of the same sex. Offenders convicted for a third time can face up to life imprisonment or the death penalty.

2SLGBTQI+ persons should carefully consider the risks of travelling to Sudan.
