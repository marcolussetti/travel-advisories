# Safety and security

## Safety and security

### Border restrictions with the Democratic Republic of the Congo

Due to the outbreak of Ebola disease, crossing between the DRC and Uganda is restricted for most travellers until further notice.

More information on the Ebola outbreaks in [Uganda](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/travel-health-notices/550) and in the [DRC](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/travel-health-notices/549)

### Border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The volatile security situation in the eastern part of neighbouring Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) could lead to possible incursions into western Uganda by armed rebel groups from the DRC.

Uganda and the DRC regularly conduct joint military operations against armed groups in North Kivu and Ituri provinces of the DRC, near Virunga National Park.

Ugandan military troops are present on both sides of the border. There is also a risk of banditry and inter-ethnic violence in this area.

### Border with South Sudan

The border with South Sudan is porous, allowing criminal and armed groups to operate in the area. Inter-communal tensions and clashes are also frequent in this region.

### Karamoja Province

Clashes between local communities occur, especially in districts north of Lake Kyoga. There is also a risk of banditry.

### Terrorism

There is a threat of terrorism in Uganda, including in Kampala.

Terrorist attacks have occurred in the past in:

* Kampala
* Kamwenge, Kasese, Kyankwanzi, and Buikwe districts
* Queen Elizabeth National Park
* the border area with the DRC

Foreigners have been targeted and further attacks cannot be ruled out.

Targets could include:

* national parks
* schools, including universities
* public areas and tourist attractions, including:

+ restaurants
+ bars
+ coffee shops
+ shopping centres
+ markets
+ hotels and other sites frequented by foreigners

* government buildings, including police stations
* places of worship
* airports and other transportation hubs and networks
* cultural venues

Be particularly vigilant during:

* sporting events
* religious holidays
* public celebrations
* major political events, such as elections

Terrorists may use such occasions to mount attacks.

Always be aware of your surroundings when in public places and stay at hotels that have robust security measures.

### National parks and wildlife reserves

There are several national parks in Uganda, including near the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Local authorities have enhanced security measures in these areas, but tourists have been involved in security incidents in the past.

National parks and wildlife reserves are in remote areas with limited emergency services. Wild animals often venture within boundaries of camps.

If you decide to visit parks and reserves:

* only use reputable and professional guides or tour operators
* do not take any tours that cross into the Democratic Republic of the Congo
* follow the advice and warnings of local tour guides and rangers
* stay informed of recent developments in the security situation in the area before travelling as it can change quickly
* do not walk around at night
* never leave children unattended

[Uganda’s National Parks and Reserves](http://www.ugandawildlife.org/) – Ugandan Wildlife Authority

### Wildlife observation

Wild animals can be dangerous, particularly if you are on foot or at close range.

* Always maintain a safe distance when observing wildlife
* Only exit a vehicle when a professional guide or warden says it’s safe to do so
* Closely follow park regulations and rangers’ advice

### Crime

There is a high level of crime throughout Uganda, particularly in urban and border areas.

#### Petty crime

Petty crime, such as pickpocketing, purse snatching and theft, including from hotel rooms, occurs frequently. Crime increases significantly after dark.

* Keep your personal belongings, including your passport and other travel documents, in a secure place
* Avoid showing signs of affluence or wearing expensive jewellery
* Do not visibly display electronics
* Avoid all unnecessary travel after dark
* Do not leave valuables or bags unattended
* Be wary of tourist excursions and only sign up with a reputable tour company
* Travel in groups if possible

#### Violent crime

Violent crime occurs frequently throughout the country and tourists have been targeted. Incidents include:

* armed robberies
* burglaries
* home invasions
* carjackings
* assaults, including sexual assault

During your stay:

* stay in accommodations with adequate security measures
* keep doors and windows locked at all times
* keep valuables out of sight and reach
* avoid isolated areas
* be extra cautious when withdrawing cash from ATMs
* do not resist an attack

### Demonstrations

Demonstrations may occur, particularly during election periods. Even peaceful demonstrations can turn violent at any time. They can also lead to disruptions to traffic and public transportation.

* Avoid areas where demonstrations and large gatherings are taking place
* Follow the instructions of local authorities
* Monitor local media for information on ongoing demonstrations

[Mass gatherings (large-scale events)](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/mass-gatherings)

### 2SLGBTQI+ persons

Ugandan law criminalizes sexual acts between individuals of the same sex and prohibits the “promotion of homosexuality.” Penalties include:

* up to life imprisonment for sexual acts between individuals of the same sex
* up to 20 years’ imprisonment for the “promotion of homosexuality”
* the death penalty for those convicted of “aggravated homosexuality”

Discrimination based on identity and sexual orientation is widespread. Incidences of blackmail and extortion directed against 2SLGBTQI+ persons and their families are common. Attacks have increased since the Parliament passed an “anti-homosexuality” bill in 2023.

2SLGBTQI+ persons are routinely harassed by security forces, which sometimes carry out arbitrary arrests and searches based on sexual orientation or gender identity. Interventions include:

* detentions
* interrogations
* alleged physical abuse

2SLGBTQI+ travellers should carefully consider the risks of travelling to Uganda.

[Travel and your sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression and sex characteristics](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/lgbt-travel)

Women travelling alone may be subject to harassment and verbal abuse.

### Forced marriage

Some Canadians have been forced into marriage against their will and without their consent in Uganda. Family and/or community members have used emotional pressure, threats, abduction, or even physical violence to coerce individuals to marry in Uganda. Sometimes, the affected individual doesn’t know in advance about the planned marriage, but once they arrive in the country, their passport and money may be taken to prevent them from returning to Canada.

**Forced to travel and remain abroad**

Some Canadians have reported being forced to travel or to remain in Uganda against their will. Once abroad, family members have confiscated the person’s passport and cellphone and withheld money to prevent them from returning to Canada. In some cases, family members have used deception or coercion to admit individuals and forcibly confine them in certain facilities claiming to offer medical or cultural "rehabilitation" services. These facilities may operate with little or no licensing or oversight, and incidents of abuse have been reported.

**If you’re in Canada**

If you’re in Canada and believe that you’re being forced to travel to Uganda to marry and/or to be left abroad against your will, you may want to consider the following safety measures:

* try to avoid or delay your travel
* share your concerns and travel details with a trusted person, such as a teacher, counsellor, friend or neighbour who can help you find support
* maintain control over your passport and other important documents
* contact your local police for immediate assistance

**If you’re in Uganda**

If you’re in Uganda and believe that you’re being forced to marry or are being kept abroad against your will and prevented from returning to Canada:

* contact the nearest office of the Government of Canada
* contact the 24/7 [Emergency Watch and Response Centre](https://travel.gc.ca/assistance/emergency-assistance)

[Forced marriage](https://travel.gc.ca/assistance/emergency-info/forced-marriage)

### Spiked food, drinks and other items

Never leave your food or drinks unattended or in the care of strangers. Avoid accepting snacks, beverages, gum or cigarettes from new acquaintances, as these items may contain drugs that could put you at risk of sexual assault and robbery.

### Roads

#### Road safety

There are major road safety issues in Uganda. Fatal accidents are common and often involve motorcyclists as well as pedestrians. Police and emergency services are limited or even unavailable in rural areas.

#### Road conditions

Road conditions vary throughout the country and at different times of the year. Driving conditions may be hazardous during the rainy seasons, from March to May and from September to November. A four-wheel-drive vehicle may be necessary to travel outside cities, off major highways and during the rainy season.

Roads are poorly maintained and can be dangerous due to:

* potholes
* stray animals
* lack of traffic signs
* pedestrians on the road
* insufficient lighting and vehicles not using their headlights
* poorly maintained vehicles

#### Driving habits

Drivers often disregard traffic laws and speeding is common. Pedestrians and other drivers’ rights of way are not respected. The Jinja-Kampala and Masaka-Kampala roads are particularly prone to traffic accidents. Drinking and driving is common, particularly at night.

If you choose to drive in Uganda:

* always drive defensively
* avoid driving after dark
* always keep doors and windows closed and locked
* never leave personal belongings unattended in a vehicle, even in the trunk
* fill your gas tank before you travel
* during the rainy season, confirm with local authorities that the chosen route is passable

#### Checkpoints

Police checkpoints are common throughout the country, especially when travelling toward western Uganda.

* Be prepared to show your identification
* Follow the instructions of the police
* Remain calm and courteous

### Public transportation

#### Buses

Avoid intercity buses, especially overnight long-distance buses, and minibuses, locally known as “matatus,” as they are often involved in fatal accidents.

#### Taxis and ride-sharing services

Taxi availability may be limited outside city centres.

* Avoid using motorcycle taxis, locally known as “boda-boda” or three-wheel taxis called “tuk-tuks,” as they are frequently involved in accidents
* Only use licensed taxis selected by a reputable hotel or restaurant, or one located at an official taxi stand
* Never share a taxi with strangers
* Make sure the driver doesn’t pick up other passengers on the way to your destination
* Ask the driver to start the meter or negotiate the fare in advance
* Have small bills ready for payment

Ride-sharing services are available.

* Use a trusted ride-sharing application
* Confirm the driver’s identity and licence plate before getting into the car
* Do not share your ride with another person
* Use a security feature to share your ride status with a contact

#### Ferries

Fatal ferry accidents have occurred due to poor maintenance, overcrowding, and a lack of safety measures and equipment.

If you decide to travel by ferry:

* only use a reputable company
* make sure appropriate safety equipment is available
* do not board a boat that is too crowded or in poor condition
* avoid water transportation during adverse weather conditions
