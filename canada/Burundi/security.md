# Safety and security

## Safety and security

### Security situation in Bujumbura Mairie Province

The security situation in Bujumbura Mairie Province is volatile and unpredictable due to the ongoing conflict in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

If you are in Bujumbura Mairie Province or the surrounding area:

* be vigilant
* follow the instructions of local authorities

### Bubanza and Cibitoke provinces and Kibira National Park

Border areas in Bubanza and Cibitoke provinces and Kibira National Park may be affected by spillover from ongoing conflicts in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), military operations, and cross-border paramilitary and criminal activity. The risk of armed attacks and kidnappings is high in these areas.

Land border crossings with the DRC may close at short notice due to the volatile security situation. Those with Rwanda are closed.

### Political tensions

Burundi is experiencing recurring episodes of political violence involving the use of small arms and grenades, including in Bujumbura.

### Security searches, roadblocks and identification

Security forces have the authority to conduct searches at any time in homes and vehicles, allegedly to recover weapons. Vehicle searches are usually conducted at roadblocks set up throughout the country, including in Bujumbura.

To search a house, officers must present their identification card, but no search warrant is required. Cooperate with the authorities in the event of a search. If you encounter problems, request to contact the High Commission of Canada in Rwanda, in Kigali.

Local authorities may request to see your identification documents at any time.

* Always carry a photocopy of your photo identification
* Keep a photocopy of your passport in a safe place in case it’s lost or seized
* Keep a digital copy of your identification and travel documents

### Crime

#### Petty crime

Petty crime, such as pickpocketing and bag snatching, occurs frequently.

Theft is common in:

* hotel rooms
* vehicles
* crowded places, such as markets

During your stay in Burundi:

* keep your personal belongings, including your passport and your other travel documents, in a secure place
* avoid unnecessary travel after dark
* avoid showing signs of affluence or wearing expensive jewellery
* do not carry large sums of cash or valuables unnecessarily
* keep your vehicle’s doors and windows closed and locked at all times

### Violent crime

Violent crime occurs frequently throughout the country, including in Bujumbura and on the roads around Lake Tanganyika. Incidents include:

* armed robberies
* burglaries
* carjackings
* assaults

During your stay:

* stay in accommodations with adequate security measures
* use only reputable companies for long-term accommodations
* keep your accommodation and vehicle doors and windows closed and locked at all times
* keep valuable objects out of sight and reach
* avoid isolated areas
* be extra cautious when withdrawing cash from ATMs
* do not resist an attack

### Kidnapping

Most kidnappings are committed for ransom. They occur frequently and can occur anywhere in the country. The risk of kidnapping is high in the provinces of Bubanza and Cibitoke, as well as in Bujumbura Mairie. These crimes, often committed by armed groups, target both Burundian nationals and foreigners.

### Water and fuel shortages

During the long dry season from June to September, water shortages can occur throughout the country, including in Bujumbura.

Fuel shortages occur regularly and can last for extended periods. This can lead to reduced essential services and long lines at gas stations.

* Always keep your gas tank at least half full
* Ensure access to water and fuel
* Monitor local media to stay informed about the situation

### Power outages

Power outages occur regularly, especially outside of Bujumbura. They can last for long periods of time. Buildings are not generally equipped with generators.

Power outages could impact essential services, such as:

* the public water supply
* telecommunications
* banking transactions

### Telecommunications

The telecommunications network is not always reliable, especially in remote areas.

### Demonstrations

Prior authorization from the authorities is required for any demonstration. Demonstrations occur frequently, especially in Bujumbura. Even peaceful demonstrations can turn violent at any time. They can also lead to disruptions to traffic and public transportation.

* Avoid areas where demonstrations and large gatherings are taking place
* Follow the instructions of local authorities
* Monitor local media for information on ongoing demonstrations

[Mass gatherings (large-scale events)](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/mass-gatherings)

### National parks and nature reserves

Many national parks and nature reserves are in remote areas with limited emergency services.

If you decide to visit national parks and reserves:

* use only reputable and professional guides or tour operators
* follow the advice and warnings of local tour guides and camp employees
* do not walk around at night
* never leave children unattended

### Travelling in remote areas

Tourist infrastructure, including hotels, restaurants, and transportation networks, is limited outside major cities. The telecommunications network operates intermittently, particularly outside Bujumbura.

* Travel only during daylight outside of urban centres
* Keep a supply of water, food, fuel and an emergency kit on hand
* Bring reliable means of communication
* Use reputable tour operators

### Women’s safety

Sexual assaults, including rape, are frequent in Burundi. Pervasive staring, verbal abuse, inappropriate touching and other forms of sexual harassment may occur anywhere. Local authorities do not always respond adequately to reports of sexual violence and harassment.

* Always remain vigilant
* Do not accept offers of transportation from strangers
* Avoid walking alone after dark

[Advice for women travellers](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/advice-for-women-travellers "Advice for women travellers")

### 2SLGBTQI+ persons

Burundian law criminalizes sexual acts and relationships between persons of the same sex.

Convicted offenders could face up to 2 years of imprisonment.

2SLGBTQI+ persons should carefully consider the risks of travelling to Burundi.

[Travel and your sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression and sex characteristics](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/lgbt-travel)

### Roads

#### Road safety

There are major road safety issues in Burundi. Fatal accidents are common and often involve pedestrians. Police and emergency services assistance is limited or unavailable in rural areas.

#### Road conditions

Road conditions vary throughout the country. During the rainy season, usually from February to mid-May, driving can become hazardous due to road conditions deteriorating during flash floods.

Roads are poorly maintained and can be dangerous due to:

* potholes
* stray animals
* numerous motorcycles and bicycles
* lack of traffic signs
* pedestrians on the road
* insufficient lighting and vehicles not using their headlights
* poorly maintained vehicles

#### Driving habits

Drivers often disregard traffic laws, and speeding is common. Pedestrians and other drivers’ rights of way are not respected.

Consider hiring a car with a driver instead of driving yourself if you are not familiar with local conditions.

If you choose to drive in Burundi:

* always drive defensively
* avoid driving after dark outside of urban centres
* always keep doors and windows closed and locked
* consider using a four-wheel-drive vehicle outside urban centres
* during the rainy season, confirm with local authorities that the chosen route is passable

Traffic accidents may attract large crowds that could become hostile. Remain calm if a dispute arises. If you do not feel safe, go to the nearest police station.

### Checkpoints

There are frequent checkpoints throughout the country, especially in urban areas and along major roads.

* Be prepared to show your identification
* Follow the instructions of security forces
* Remain calm and courteous

Some individuals impersonate law enforcement officers and set up unofficial roadblocks to solicit money.

Be vigilant in remote areas.

### Public transportation

Avoid using public transportation due to overcrowding, poor vehicle maintenance, and the risk of theft and armed attacks.

#### Taxis

Most taxis and vehicles associated with ride-sharing services are unsafe. Passengers are frequently mugged or robbed by drivers.

Avoid using these means of transportation.

However, if you must use a taxi or a ride-sharing application:

* avoid hailing taxis on the street
* only use reputable taxi companies through establishments such as hotels
* confirm the driver's identity and licence plate before getting into the car
* note the driver’s name on their photo identification card and licence number
* never share a taxi with strangers
* make sure the driver does not pick up other passengers on the way to your destination
* confirm the fare in advance and have small bills available for payment

#### Buses

Bus drivers don’t always respect traffic laws.

Buses are poorly maintained and overcrowded, and theft is frequent.

### Air travel

We don't make assessments on the compliance of foreign domestic airlines with international safety standards.
